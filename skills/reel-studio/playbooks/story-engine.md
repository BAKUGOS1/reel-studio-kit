# Story engine: from a feature or an occasion to a script worth making

**Why:** reels can look premium and still fail when the idea is a setup without tension, the lines read like copy, and the narration is written for the eye. This playbook runs before every reel (concept, story reel, POV, SaaS demo, festival). **No Codex prompt, art, voice or code until the owner approves the step 5 output.**

It merges the idea and tension method from **creative-director** (Serge Shima, CC BY 4.0, `guides/creative-director/`), the first-2-seconds rules from **hook-archetypes** (MIT, `guides/hooks/`), and short borrowings credited inline. **Step 7 (narration for the ear) is our own.**

Every step writes something down in `src/<slug>/story.md`, under a heading per step. The examples below come from a wedding-planning brand for Indian families; swap in your own world.

---

## Step 0. Brief card
- Feature or occasion · format (concept / story reel / POV / SaaS demo / greeting) · length · language (English, Hindi, Hinglish).
- **The one takeaway sentence.** If it doesn't fit in one sentence, it isn't a reel yet.
- **The target emotion**, Tier 2 or 3 (`guides/creative-director/emotion-hierarchy.md`). Tier 1 ("happy", "sad", "relief") is rejected.
  Good: "wry affection for Dad's diary", "bittersweet pride at retiring it", "the small shame of not knowing who paid".
- The claim source, if the reel shows a feature (from `references/brand-context.md`).

## Step 1. Insight (before any idea)
1. **Tension spotting** (`guides/creative-director/insight-mining.md` §3): list 5 things your category, its experts and your audience always say. For each,
   ask "but what actually happens?" Keep a gap that makes people **wince and nod** at once.
2. **The insight, in one sentence:** "[audience member] wants X, but Y stands in the way, because Z." It must pass "yes, exactly…
   but I never thought of it that way" (the insight quality test, §7).
3. **Real words.** 3-5 phrases real people say: group-chat lines, comments under your reels, your own experience, DMs.
   Note where each came from. A script with no real voice is marked UNVALIDATED.
4. **Specifics** a viewer hasn't thought of: turmeric on the diary page, a total crossed out three times, the ₹4,000 envelope,
   the drummer's advance, "Uncle gave 50… or was it 70?". Never invent an amount; write `[NEED: real amount]`.

## Step 2. Eight or more concepts that really differ
Use one method from each of three families (`guides/creative-director/methods-catalog.md`):
- **Structural:** SIT subtraction or substitution ("a wedding with no diary"; "the diary is the guest of honour").
- **Collision:** bisociation (the account diary as an heirloom, a crime file, a will, a love letter, a court exhibit).
- **Inversion:** worst idea, reverse brainstorm, provocation ("Don't download us if…").

Vary the frame across the set: argument (what-is / what-could-be), character story, POV, **an object as the character**, and a calm
kishotenketsu piece with no villain (festivals). **Number them; ideas 1-3 are warm-up clichés**, so the pick must be idea 4 or later,
or it must clearly beat them (Pixar rule 6, serial-order effect). One sentence each, plus the tension it holds open.

## Step 3. Kill round, then pick one
- Score each 1-5 on originality, fit, emotion tier, whether the product moment is real, and whether it fits the time.
- **Competitor test:** if a competitor, an adjacent service or a bank could run it with its own name, originality is capped at 2.
- **Gap check:** "clever but doesn't matter" → add human stakes; "matters but boring" → add a surprise.
- **Viewer panel:** your three real viewers (for example a father scrolling at 11 pm, the bride's sister, a strategist): "would I forward this to the family group?"
- Take the top 2, argue against each in one line (pre-mortem), pick one, and write its **concept card**: name, the pain, the wished-for
  state, the angle, two lines, why it could work.

## Step 4. Find the moment, build backward
- Write the **five-second moment** first: the one realization the reel exists for (Matthew Dicks, *Storyworthy*).
- Write the **last line first** (Pixar rule 3).
- Pick the shape (`guides/creative-director/storytelling-frameworks.md`):
  - concept / argument reel → **Sparkline**: what-is / what-could-be twice, then the bridge;
  - story reel → **Story Spine** (30 s: lines 1-2 in 5-7 s, the "because of that" chain in 15-18 s, the end in 5-7 s);
  - festival or warm piece → **kishotenketsu** (intro, development, twist, reconciliation; no villain).
- **Phoenix head, pig belly, leopard tail:** a striking open, a middle that builds, a turn like thunder, a short tail that lingers.

## Step 5. The three lines (the owner's approval gate)
1. **Problem:** stakes in the first line (Dicks' "elephant") plus one sensory detail.
2. **Turn:** an action or a revelation, never more explanation, held by **one planned silence** (Dicks' "hourglass").
3. **Payoff:** more than the setup promised, in a way the viewer didn't expect. The **person is the hero, the product is the tool**
   (StoryBrand). The last image **echoes or reverses** the first: diary open at the start, diary closed at the end.

Each line links to the next with **BUT** or **THEREFORE**, never "and then" (Parker & Stone). Show these three lines, the insight,
the format, the emotion and the test question to the owner. Wait for their yes.

## Step 6. Hooks (the first 2 seconds, three channels)
- Draft **6 hooks**: 3 types × 2 each, from different families (`guides/hooks/hook-archetypes.md`): one contrarian or intellectual,
  one scene or POV, one aimed straight at the viewer.
- Each pulls **two levers** (e.g. a scene plus a broken prediction).
- **Triad:** the voice line, the on-screen text and the first frame carry **different** information; the text never captions the voice.
- Frame 0 moves. Never a logo or a static card. Spoken hook under 2 s; on-screen text 3-7 words.
- The line after the hook extends the hook's own premise, and a **confirmation beat at 2-5 s** proves it's real.

## Step 7. Narration for the ear (our own method)
**Build the narrator first:** who speaks (a sister, a father, a calm outsider, the diary itself), what they know cold, and one opinion that
costs them something. A narrator with no opinion is invisible (Pixar rule 7).

**Word budget:** about 150 words a minute in English and 140 in Hinglish, minus the silence. A 35-40 s reel takes **75-90 words**.
Over budget means cut, never speed up.

**Line rules:**
- One thought per line, 3-9 words, one breath. Subject, verb, object; no clause before the main point.
- **End-weight:** the heavy word goes last. "The accounts are still… in the diary."
- Numbers as people say them: "three lakh", not "₹3,00,000". At most one number a reel, two in a story reel.
- **Code-switch on purpose** (for mixed-language audiences): the home language for feeling and family (diary, hisaab, shagun, Papa), English for the modern or digital (online,
  app, invite). The switch itself carries the contrast or the joke.
- **Three, then break:** three short parallel lines set a pattern and the fourth breaks it ("Outfit — online. Venue — online.
  Card — on WhatsApp. And the accounts…"). That break is the setup for the turn.
- **Ration the punch:** two punch lines only, the hook and the turn. Every other line stays plain, so the silence lands.
- One deliberate fragment, at the turn.
- **Dinner test:** it sounds like a slightly lifted version of telling it at the dinner table. No announcer voice (Dicks).
- **Loop:** the last line can flow back into the first frame.
- Banned: "not just X, it's Y", "imagine a world", "because your family deserves", "say goodbye to", wisdom-shaped endings, and any
  closing line another brand could end its ad with.

**Mark up each line for the voice and the edit** (goes into `voice.md` and the beat sheet):
`[t 0.0-2.1] VO: "…" | delivery: dry, half-smile | stress: DIARY | ON-SCREEN: "…" | VISUAL: … | CUT ON: "diary"`

- **One planned silence**, 0.8-1.5 s, before the turn. Something moves during it (a slow push, a page settling), so it reads as an
  interrupt, not dead air.
- Pauses for ElevenLabs: follow the text-to-speech skill (break tags where the model supports them); never runs of "…".
- Hindi lines follow `references/voice-hindi.md` (Devanagari, English family words in Devanagari, no brand word the voice can't say).

## Step 8. Beat sheet and the three tests
- Three tracks: time / VISUAL / ON-SCREEN TEXT / SPOKEN.
- **Mute test:** the on-screen text alone, worded differently from the voice, still carries the argument.
- **Cut test:** every line grabs, advances or sets up the payoff, or it goes.
- **Value-turn test:** mark each beat + or − at its start and end. A beat whose charge doesn't change is cut or merged (McKee). Never
  three beats of the same tone in a row.

## Step 9. Read aloud, then score
Read it aloud at real speed and time it. Score it with the rubric below. Fix the lowest score, one change at a time, and re-score.
After two passes below the bar, go back to step 3 and take the second concept. **Don't polish a weak idea.**

After posting, read the retention curve: a drop at 0-2 s means the hook or frame 0; a mid-reel cliff means dead air or a thin body;
a bump at the end means people loop.

## The rubric

### Hard gates (any NO sends the script back)
| # | Gate | Back to |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | The takeaway fits one sentence, and the insight fits "wants X, but Y, because Z" | Step 1 |
| G2 | The chosen concept is idea 4 or later, or clearly beat the warm-ups | Step 2 |
| G3 | Competitor test: the hook or the turn would NOT work with another brand's name | Step 3 |
| G4 | Every number and detail is real or sourced; no `[NEED]` left | Step 1 |
| G5 | A person is the hero and the product is the tool | Step 5 |
| G6 | Within the word budget at real speed, with exactly one planned silence | Step 7 |

### Scored (0-3 each, 36 max). Ship at **28+** with no score of 0 or 1.
| # | Question | 0 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Insight:** would your viewer say "yes, exactly, but I never thought of it that way"? | A fact everyone knows | A truth felt but never said |
| 2 | **Tension:** wince and nod, held open until the turn? | Mild, or settled early | Uncomfortable, recognisable, held |
| 3 | **Emotion tier** | Tier 1 or none | Tier 3, and the read-aloud produces it |
| 4 | **Specificity:** can you picture every line? | "memories", "chaos", "manage" | Objects, names, amounts, places |
| 5 | **Hook:** read cold, do you want line 2? Two levers, triad split? | Throat-clearing, text = voice | Two levers, three channels |
| 6 | **Causality:** BUT / THEREFORE, and every beat turns | "And then" lists | Every beat changes something |
| 7 | **The turn:** action or revelation after the silence | Announced or explained | Short, inevitable, surprising |
| 8 | **Payoff beyond the promise,** last image echoes or reverses the first | Feature list, tidy moral | Echo or reversal plus the real screen |
| 9 | **Ear:** short lines, end-weight, spoken numbers, deliberate code-switch | Written for reading | One breath a line, sounds like a person |
| 10 | **Punch rationing, no clichés** | 3+ banned phrases | None, and only two punch lines |
| 11 | **Mute and cut tests** | Meaningless muted, padding | Holds muted, nothing to cut |
| 12 | **The memorable line:** one line a viewer would repeat to a friend | None | Quotable, and only your brand could say it |

### Instant deductions (−3 each)
- Frame 0 is a logo or a static card.
- The voice and the on-screen text say the same words.
- A product claim with no source.
- A second silence, or dead air that wasn't planned.
- The script skipped the owner's step 5 approval.
- The closing line could end any brand's ad.
